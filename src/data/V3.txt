term|definition
Guard|Position where the bottom player uses legs to control and attack.
Closed guard|Legs locked around opponent waist, bottom player on back.
Open guard|Guard with legs not locked, using feet/legs as frames.
Half guard|One of your legs traps one of theirs; commonly bottom position.
Knee shield|Bottom-side knee across opponent torso as a frame.
Dogfight|Transitional half-guard position, both partners on knees/elbows.
Underhook|Arm placed under opponent armpit for inside control.
Overhook|Arm placed over opponent arm; generally inferior to underhook.
Whizzer|Overhook used defensively to prevent a takedown or come-up.
Crossface|Forearm across opponent jaw/neck to control head posture.
Seatbelt / Harness|Over-the-shoulder/under-arm grip configuration for back control.
Hooks|Feet inserted around opponent thighs (often from back control).
Frame|Skeletal structure used to resist pressure and create space.
Shrimp / Hip escape|Moving hips away from opponent to create space.
Technical stand-up|Standing up from seated while keeping a hand up defensively.
Sprawl|Kicking legs back to defend a leg attack by flattening hips on opponent.
Snapdown|Pulling opponent head down to break posture or expose the back.
Arm drag|Pulling opponent arm across body to get to an angle or back.
Guard pull|Intentionally sitting/pulling to a bottom guard position.
Kimura|Reverse shoulder lock using a figure-four grip.
Americana|Shoulder lock with a forward-rotated figure-four grip.
Armbar|Straight-arm lock isolating the elbow.
Triangle|Choke using legs around neck and one trapped arm.
Omoplata|Shoulder lock using legs to rotate opponent shoulder.
Guillotine|Front choke applied to the neck from front headlock or guard.
Anaconda|Front choke threaded under the far armpit, finished by rolling.
Darce|Front choke threaded under the near armpit, finished from side.
Bow-and-arrow|Collar choke from back using collar + leg/pant grip.
Rear naked choke (RNC)|No-gi blood choke from back control.
Paper cutter|Collar choke from side control using forearm pressure.
S-mount|Mount variation with one leg bent by opponent ear, used for armbars.
Gift wrap|Trapping opponent arm across their own face from mount or side.
Knee-on-belly|Transitional pin with one knee across opponent torso.
Knee cut|Passing technique sliding knee across opponent thigh.
Leg weave|Pass threading arm between opponent legs to stabilize.
Backstep|Passing technique stepping trapped leg back and around.
DLR (De la Riva)|Open-guard hook behind opponent far knee.
SLX (Single-leg X)|Leg-entangled guard isolating one leg of opponent.
Old school sweep|Half-guard sweep controlling far ankle from underhook.
Granby|Rolling/inverted escape, often from turtle or knees.
Turtle|Defensive position on hands and knees, chest down.
Pull-through / Run the pipe|Single-leg finish by circling opponent base leg.
Position hierarchy|Ranking of positions by control (back > mount > side > half > guard).
